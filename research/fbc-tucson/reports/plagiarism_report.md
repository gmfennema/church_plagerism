# Sermon Dependence Review

**Fellowship Bible Church Tucson × Alistair Begg, _A Study in 1 Thessalonians_**

**Prepared:** September 7, 2026
**Purpose:** Evidence review of textual dependence, not a legal or ecclesiastical verdict.

The authoritative formatted report is [`plagiarism_report.pdf`](plagiarism_report.pdf). The final machine-readable
analysis is [`sermon_dependence_analysis.json`](sermon_dependence_analysis.json).

## Summary

Six of the nine reviewed FBC Tucson recordings are sermons by senior pastor Pat McClanahan in the 2026
_A Church of Irresistible Influence_ series. Each of the six maps one-to-one onto Alistair Begg's sermon
on the same passage, usually under the same title.

- **27.2%** of the six sermon bodies falls inside near-verbatim reused regions: 8,943 of 32,920 words.
- **25.0%** remains after regions classified as shared Scripture are removed.
- Individual sermon-body coverage ranges from **10% to 40%**.
- **6 of 6** sermons follow a same-titled Begg sermon.
- **0 of 6** sermons name Alistair Begg, Truth For Life, or Parkside Church.
- Reviewers catalogued **119 borrowed non-Scripture elements**, including illustrations, commentator
  quotations, prayers, wordplay, applications, transitions, and structural devices.

## Why the preliminary 3.72% figure was too low

The earlier diagnostic counted only uninterrupted exact runs of eight or more words and divided them by the
entire livestream transcript. Songs, announcements, and the altar call made up roughly one-third to one-half
of those transcripts. Caption errors and light paraphrase also broke otherwise continuous reused passages.

The final review instead:

1. marks the start and end of the sermon proper;
2. aligns near-verbatim passages using exact seeds followed by token-level rescoring;
3. classifies every reused region as Scripture, mixed, or non-Scripture; and
4. checks the full transcripts for spoken attribution.

The old **3.72% whole-service exact-run diagnostic is not the sermon-body overlap figure**. The final
sermon-body figure is **27.2% overall, with a 10% to 40% range across the six sermons**.

## Guardrails

The review establishes textual dependence from public transcripts: what was said, in what order, and whether
the source was named. It does not establish intent and does not assess whether Truth For Life's usage terms
permit re-preaching. Both local and source transcripts are machine-generated, so word-level estimates are
best understood as conservative.
