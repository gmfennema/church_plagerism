Tucson Sermon Review

What this is

A public, evidence-first look at sermons preached in Tucson. For each church we ask two simple questions about its regular preachers:

  • Copying. Do their sermons repeat another preacher’s published sermons word for word, without saying so?
  • AI writing. Has their writing style changed in ways associated with AI-generated text?

Transcripts of public sermons are compared word for word against suspected sources. Runs of eight or more identical words are counted, Scripture and liturgy are set aside, and the longest passages are read in context. AI-writing signals are only called a concern when the same preacher’s older sermons are available for comparison.

Every finding links to the underlying report so anyone can check the work. This is a review of evidence, not a verdict.

What the labels mean

  • Concerns foundThe evidence met the project’s threshold. Read the details before drawing conclusions.
  • No concerns foundEnough sermons were reviewed and nothing notable turned up.
  • InconclusiveSomething was reviewed, but there is not enough evidence either way.
  • Review underwayResearch is active; nothing has been published yet.
  • Not yet reviewedThe church is on the map, but nobody has reviewed its sermons.

“Inconclusive” does not mean “probably concerning.” A dashed ring on the map means the pin is approximate.
